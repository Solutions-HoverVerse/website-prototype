Hoververse / Why Hoververse

Why Hoververse is different.

Physical AI and spatial computing are maturing fast, with Apple, Meta, NVIDIA and others racing to build it into personal devices and autonomous machines. Unlike any of them, Hoververse is focused on shared and public use cases — the spaces people occupy together, not the ones they carry or wear alone. Hoververse understands physical intent — a proprietary stack spanning computer vision, edge computing and interaction design, positioned at the intersection of AI and physical space.

The third wave

Input hasn't changed in 40 years. Change is coming.

Computing has moved in eras — mainframe, desktop, then mobile — each unlocking a bigger market than the last. Spatial computing is the next era, and it breaks free from the screen entirely.

1980s
Desktop

Point, click and type

The desktop paradigm — tethered to a physical device at a desk.

2007
Mobile

Touch, anywhere

Mobile freed computing from the desk and made touching a screen the universal interface.

2027
Spatial

Gesture in the air

The next shift of the same magnitude — gesture-based computing with no device, no surface to touch.

"An evolution from mobile computing into spatial computing … the kind of change that came with the smartphone." — Cathy Hackl, futurist & author of "Spatial Computing"

Two problems · one root cause

Both trace back to touching a shared surface.

Stagnation

Keyboard, mouse and GUI have barely changed since the 1980s. Mobile didn't disrupt input — it just made touching a screen universal. Every industry fix is incremental; no one removed the input device.

Contamination

Shared keyboards, mice and kiosks are among a hospital's dirtiest surfaces. HAIs affect ~1 in 31 patients (CDC); every touch is a transmission event. Wipes and coatings treat the symptom, not the surface.

The root cause: physical contact with a shared surface. Spatial, touchless input removes it — and Hover OS is the platform built for that shift.
